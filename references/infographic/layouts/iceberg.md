# Iceberg

**Best for**: Showing hidden complexity, known vs unknown, visible vs invisible layers
**Structure**: An iceberg visual with a visible tip above a waterline and a much larger submerged portion below. The waterline divides the visible/obvious from the hidden/underlying elements
**Elements**: Iceberg tip (above water), submerged mass (below water), waterline divider, sky/water background context, labels on both portions

## Content Requirements
- A clear distinction between what is visible/surface-level and what is hidden/deep
- Typically: 1-3 items above the waterline (the "tip"), 3-8 items below (the "mass")
- The submerged portion should represent the bulk of complexity, effort, or importance
- Waterline metaphor must be appropriate for the content

## When to Use
- Explaining hidden costs, complexity, or technical debt
- Surface-level symptoms vs root causes
- Known risks vs unknown/unmanaged risks
- Conscious vs subconscious factors in psychology/behavior

## When NOT to Use
- When the visible/hidden dichotomy does not hold (everything is known)
- When you need precise proportions — the iceberg is a metaphor, not a chart
- When the "hidden" items are actually just "more items" with no qualitative difference
